# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. **Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: persistent/minimizable sessions and rest state refresh a familiar logger pattern (`01-evidence.md#1-innovative`).
   Justification: the implementation improves established workout patterns, but does not introduce a pattern demonstrably absent from peer products.

2. **Good design makes a product useful — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: set logging is direct, but the core workout is long and session-note authoring is absent (`01-evidence.md#2-useful`).
   Justification: the primary logging task completes, while adjacent information and note flows add friction or remain unsupported.

3. **Good design is aesthetic — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: the palette is coherent, but the surface uses 17 spacing values, 16 type sizes, and several sub-12px labels (`01-evidence.md#3-aesthetic`).
   Justification: the incumbent world is recognizable, yet more than five visible scale inconsistencies prevent a single disciplined system.

4. **Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: three data scopes are called “note/diary,” and chips alternate between metadata and action (`01-evidence.md#4-understandable`).
   Justification: multiple controls and labels require model knowledge to predict what will persist and where.

5. **Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: global navigation recedes during training, but repeated micro-controls dominate every exercise card (`01-evidence.md#5-unobtrusive`).
   Justification: chrome is quiet, although the task surface still competes with its own controls.

6. **Good design is honest — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: “complete backup” omits collections and “next time” is not session-scoped (`01-evidence.md#6-honest`).
   Justification: there are two concrete label-to-behavior inflations, even though no deceptive commercial pattern exists.

7. **Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: native controls and restrained tokens are durable, while the card/chip/tiny-mono treatment is a current convention (`01-evidence.md#7-long-lasting`).
   Justification: one dated structural marker remains inside an otherwise robust visual foundation.

8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: all state categories exist, but dialog focus, toast semantics, disabled differentiation, and two light-mode contrasts are rough (`01-evidence.md#8-thorough`).
   Justification: the state matrix is present with more than one detail needing production hardening, but fewer than four categories are missing.

9. **Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: initial JS is about 1.03MB and the 1MB catalog is eager, despite motion and theme safeguards (`01-evidence.md#9-environmentally-friendly`).
   Justification: the initial payload sits in the 500KB–2MB band and imposes avoidable work before the primary task.

10. **Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 1/3**
    Evidence: duplicate technique navigation, mixed chip roles, a long keyboard path, and undisciplined scales are removable (`01-evidence.md#10-as-little-design-as-possible`).
    Justification: at least three repeated or nonessential patterns can be removed without harming the task.

**Total: 15/30**
